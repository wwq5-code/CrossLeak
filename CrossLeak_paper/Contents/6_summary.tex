


 

\section{Summary and Future Work} \label{s_a_fw}

In this paper, we studied privacy leakage in secure-aggregation federated unlearning. We showed that even when secure aggregation hides client-level updates and gradients, the original and unlearned aggregated models can still expose information about the unlearning request. To investigate this risk, we proposed CrossLeak, a post-unlearning attack that compares paired model representations before and after unlearning. By training a sparse CrossCoder on public probe samples, CrossLeak separates shared model behavior from unlearning-sensitive features, enabling both deleted-label inference and deletion-sensitive feature inference.

Our experiments on representative federated unlearning methods and datasets show that current unlearning algorithms can leak deleted labels and sensitive visual concepts under this restricted model-comparison threat model. These results suggest that secure aggregation alone is insufficient to hide the privacy signal introduced by forgetting. Future work includes developing defenses that reduce before-after representation leakage, designing verification methods that account for leakage risks, and extending the analysis to larger models, more realistic federated deployments, and stronger privacy mechanisms such as differential privacy.
